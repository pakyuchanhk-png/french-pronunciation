About French pronunciation

French spelling and pronunciation don’t always match, which is what makes it hard for beginners. A handful of patterns cause most of the trouble: ou, oi and eu; the four nasal vowels (on, an, in, un); the throaty French r; and the accents é, è and ê. Learn these few and most French words suddenly become readable. This tool lets you hear each one as often as you like.

Frequently asked questions

How do you pronounce the French R?

The French “r” (/ʁ/) is made at the back of the throat rather than with the tip of the tongue like the English r. It sounds a little like a soft gargle. Practise with words such as rouge and Paris.

How is “ou” pronounced in French?

The combination ou makes a single /u/ sound, like “oo” in “food” — for example, bonjour and vous.

What are French nasal sounds?

Nasal vowels are made by letting air pass through the nose. French has four main ones: on/om (/ɔ̃/), an/en (/ɑ̃/), in/ain (/ɛ̃/) and un (/œ̃/), as in nom, enfant, pain and un.

What is the difference between é and è?

é (accent aigu) sounds like “ay” (/e/), as in café. è (accent grave) sounds like “eh” (/ɛ/), as in père.

Are final consonants silent in French?

Often, yes. Many final consonants — like the -s in vous or the -t in petit — are not pronounced, although there are exceptions you learn with practice.
